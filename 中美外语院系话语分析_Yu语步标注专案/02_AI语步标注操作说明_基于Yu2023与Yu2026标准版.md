# 中美高校外语院系英文简介语料库：AI 语步标注标准操作说明（SOP & Master Prompt）

> **方法论基准文献**
> 1. **Yu, D. (2023).** A cross-cultural genre analysis of leadership statements in Italian and American university sustainability reports. *IEEE Transactions on Professional Communication*, 66(1), 26–47.（确立**句子级基本分析单位**、**显性元功能优先判准**与**篇幅延展度 Extensiveness** 指标）
> 2. **Yu, D., Yu, R., Bondi, M., & Hyland, K. (2026).** Enhancing LLM-assisted move annotation: A focus on inter-model inconsistencies. *Research Methods in Applied Linguistics*, 5, 100328.（确立 **Step-by-Step 渐进式指令架构**、**整句起止标签约束**、**`<M_a><M_b>` 复合语步嵌套规则**、**`<OTHER>` 防强行归类兜底标签**、**四大模型偏见防控机制**及**双异构模型独立标注 + 差异项人工裁决工作流**）

---

## 第一部分：研究者投喂与质控操作规程（Human-in-the-Loop 工作流）

在把语料交给 AI 之前，请严格遵循 **Yu et al. (2026)** 验证过的以下 4 条黄金操作纪律（这是此前直接整篇碎切标注效果不佳的根本原因）：

1. **严守“完整句子级单位（Sentence-Level Unit）”，禁止句内碎切短语**
   - **Yu (2023, p. 32)** 与 **Yu et al. (2026, Appendix 1, p. 15)** 明确规定：**每一行只放一个完整句子，标签只能出现在完整句子的句首和句尾（`<M06>Complete sentence.</M06>`）**，严禁把同一个句子切成三四个无主谓残片。
   - 若一句包含一主一次信息（如邀请句中顺带提及专业数），按**主导交际目的**单标；**唯当**同一完整句内两个命题分量对等、缺一不可时，才使用 Yu 式嵌套双标 **`<M06><M07>Complete sentence.</M07></M06>`**（统计篇幅延展度时词数对半计入两语步）。
2. **独立新会话 + 分块投喂，杜绝“指令衰减（Instruction Degradation）”**
   - **Yu et al. (2026, p. 3)** 指出：长对话或超长单文本会导致模型逐渐遗忘边界规则。
   - 对于 `1–100` 行的高校文件，每次**新建独立会话**（Temperature 设为 `0`–`0.2`），完整粘贴下方【第二部分 Prompt】+ 原文即可；对于 `100` 行以上的多网页拼接文件（如 `Carnegie Mellon University.txt`、`Cornell`、`UT Austin`），按 `60–80` 行一段分批独立投喂。
3. **双异构模型独立盲标 + 仅核查不一致句（Dual-LLM Verification）**
   - 用同一份【第二部分 Prompt】分别投喂 **模型 A**（如 GPT / Claude）与 **模型 B**（如 Gemini / DeepSeek / Qwen），互不看对方结果。
   - **人工只需审阅两模型标签不一致的句子（约占总句数 15%–22%）**，即可在节省 80% 人工工作量的前提下将整体准确率提升至 **95%–96%**（Yu et al., 2026, Table 3 & Fig. 2）。
4. **先用 `<OTHER>` 隔离网页噪音与外院/第三方残片，再算统计分母**
   - 凡网页孤立菜单、面包屑导航、重复页脚、纯人名/职称通讯录名片、或混入的其他院系/外部合作机构自述（`"we"` 不指代目标外语院系），一律由 AI 标为 `<OTHER>...</OTHER>`，后续计算语步覆盖率与 Yu (2023) `Extensiveness (%)` 时统一从分母中剔除。

## 第二部分：直接复制投喂给 AI 的完整标注指令（Master Prompt）

> **使用方法**：每次新建空白对话，将下方 `===== BEGIN AI PROMPT =====` 至 `===== END AI PROMPT =====` 之间的全部内容完整复制，在最末尾粘贴待标注的高校文本后发送即可。

```text
===== BEGIN AI PROMPT =====
You are an expert corpus linguist and Swalesian genre analyst specializing in cross-cultural institutional discourse analysis of Chinese and American university foreign language department "About Us" / introductory web texts.

Please learn the following instructions step by step, internalize every boundary criterion and annotated example, and then complete the move annotation task strictly as instructed.

======================================================================
LEARN INSTRUCTION 1: THE 19 RHETORICAL MOVES + OTHER SCHEMA
======================================================================
You must annotate institutional texts from university foreign language departments (e.g., School of Foreign Languages, Department of Languages/Cultures/Applied Linguistics, Department of Modern/East Asian/Romance Languages) using ONLY the following 20 XML tags (`<M01>` to `<M19>`, plus `<OTHER>`).

Core Analytical Principle (Yu, 2023): Identify the move of each complete sentence according to its EXPLICIT, DOMINANT communicative purpose in context—NEVER rely blindly on isolated trigger words or sentence position.

--- DOMAIN I: Institutional Identity, History & Values ---
1. <M01> Establishing institutional identity and governance structure (界定机构定位与组织架构)
   - Core Question: Who is this department right now, what college/campus does it belong to, what internal sub-units/centers does it house, and who holds formal leadership/governance roles?
   - Includes: Current institutional nature, campus/college affiliation, list of internal departments/divisions/research centers (names only), leadership roles (Dean/Chair/Executive Committee), administrative governance duties, and formal sign-offs at the end of a Dean/Head's statement (Name + Official Title as one block).
   - Boundary: Current governance/structure = <M01>; faculty headcounts/degree ratios/facilities = <M08>; listing center names only = <M01> (explaining actual research questions = <M07>); historical founding/renaming = <M02>; rankings/honors = <M09>; bare webpage breadcrumbs or isolated department titles without predicate = <OTHER>.

2. <M02> Tracing historical continuity and heritage (追溯历史沿革与学脉传承)
   - Core Question: Where did this institution come from, and how did it evolve historically to the present?
   - Includes: Founding year, predecessor units, historical mergers, relocations, renamings, rebuildings, historical milestones (including historical launch years of degree programs narrated along a chronological timeline), and founding pioneers.
   - Boundary: Chronological past narrative = <M02>; describing currently offered degree programs/courses today = <M05>; obituaries mourning deceased individuals = <M19>; moral condemnation/repudiation of historical racism or land acknowledgements = <M03>.

3. <M03> Articulating core values and public ethical stances (宣示价值准则与公共立场)
   - Core Question: What fundamental philosophy, academic values, national/humanistic mission, or public ethical stance does the institution stand for?
   - Includes: Core institutional motto/philosophy, academic freedom/integrity principles, moral commitment to national service / human civilization / DEI (diversity, equity, inclusion), and public ethical statements condemning injustice or acknowledging Indigenous land history.
   - Boundary: Abstract institutional ethos/values = <M03>; concrete statements about what competencies students acquire or how teaching cultivates talents ("aims to cultivate talents equipped with...") = <M06>; listing courses on race/gender/social justice = <M05>; future strategic plans with explicit future time markers = <M13>.

--- DOMAIN II: Disciplinary Relevance, Education & Research ---
4. <M04> Explicating disciplinary scope and societal relevance (阐释学科内涵与时代价值)
   - Core Question: Stepping outside this specific department's own offerings, what is this discipline and why does studying languages/literatures/cultures matter in today's world?
   - Includes: General conceptual definitions of linguistics/literature/humanities and arguments linking globalization, societal challenges, or human understanding to the necessity of language/cultural study (e.g., "More and more, the world needs truly global citizens...").
   - Boundary: Subject must be the discipline or world at large—NOT "our faculty research..." (<M07>) and NOT "our courses give students..." (<M06>).

5. <M05> Presenting degree programs and curricular provision (介绍学位项目与课程供给)
   - Core Question: What specific degree programs, majors/minors, certificates, languages, and courses are currently offered to learners?
   - Includes: Current BA/MA/PhD/postdoc programs, majors, minors, combination majors, certificates, languages taught, course tiers, and specific course titles/topics offered.
   - Boundary: Objective inventory of degree/course offerings = <M05>; pedagogical methods and student skill development = <M06>; mandatory credit quotas, GPA thresholds, or administrative procedures = <M16>; external training/testing services for government/public = <M11>; enrolled student name rosters = <OTHER>.

6. <M06> Explaining educational goals and pedagogy (阐明育人目标与教学模式)
   - Core Question: What kind of talents/competencies does the department cultivate in current students, and through what teaching/learning practices?
   - Includes: Target student competencies (critical thinking, intercultural competence, communication skills, interdisciplinary/innovative literacy), classroom pedagogy (small seminars, student-centered inquiry, mentorship), experiential/co-curricular learning (e.g., undergraduate student-edited literary journals, reading groups), and interdisciplinary study experience before graduation.
   - Boundary: In-program student development before graduation = <M06>; post-graduation career fields, placement rates, graduate school destinations, or alumni career/life testimonies = <M12>.

7. <M07> Mapping institutional research agenda and specializations (界定研究议程与学术专长)
   - Core Question: What scholarly fields, historical periods, regions, theoretical questions, and interdisciplinary clusters do THIS department and its faculty actively research?
   - Includes: Research specializations, theoretical/methodological orientations, and interdisciplinary research clusters explicitly belonging to THIS target department's scholars.
   - Boundary: Must describe THIS target department's research agenda! External library/consortium scope (e.g., an external institute's collections covering medicine or art history) = <M08>; publication/grant counts or awards = <M09>; listing center names only = <M01>.

--- DOMAIN III: Resources & Reputation ---
8. <M08> Presenting faculty strength and support resources (展示师资力量与支撑资源)
   - Core Question: What human, material, archival, financial, and student-support resources enable learning and research?
   - Includes: Faculty scale, academic ranks, doctoral ratios, visiting/foreign/teaching faculty teams, student-faculty ratio, libraries, archival collections, language/VR/interpretation labs, regular scholarships/fellowships/travel grants/fee waivers available to students or scholars, and academic/advising support services.
   - Boundary: Input conditions & available support/funding = <M08>; prestigious awards won, rankings, key-discipline titles, or research output counts = <M09>; mandatory regulatory rules/deadlines = <M16>; fundraising appeals asking alumni/donors to give money = <M18>.

9. <M09> Establishing academic reputation and achievements (证明学术声誉与办学成就)
   - Core Question: What external rankings, official accreditations, cumulative outputs, awards, or landmark achievements prove the institution's excellence?
   - Includes: QS/domestic rankings, National First-Class / Key Discipline status, cumulative grant/book/paper achievements, faculty/student honors & prizes (e.g., Pulitzer Prize, Phi Beta Kappa, award-winning teachers), student course-rating acclaim, first-in-the-country pioneers, and cumulative summaries of major conferences hosted.
   - Boundary: Undated/cumulative conference or achievement summaries ("successfully hosted many international conferences") = <M09>; dated announcements of a single upcoming lecture or recent individual news item = <M17>; future aspirations = <M13>.

--- DOMAIN IV: External Engagement, Outcomes & Future Vision ---
10. <M10> Presenting institutional partnerships and international exchange (展示合作网络与国际交流)
    - Core Question: With which domestic/international universities, academic consortia, or partner institutions has the department built collaborative networks or exchange channels?
    - Includes: Inter-university agreements, international academic consortium memberships, reciprocal institutional agreements, study abroad / student exchange / 2+2 joint programs, and visiting scholar exchange networks.
    - Boundary: Institutional partnerships & study-abroad/exchange channels = <M10>; students acquiring intercultural skills inside campus courses = <M06>; one-way public service/training delivered to local society/government = <M11>.

11. <M11> Presenting public outreach and social contribution (呈现公共服务与社会贡献)
    - Core Question: How does the institution apply its expertise beyond degree-seeking campus students to serve government, K–12 education, industry, communities, or public culture?
    - Includes: Public language training/testing for officials or citizens, policy think-tank consultation, K–12 curriculum outreach, cultural dissemination, community service, and public-facing humanities periodicals engaging society at large.
    - Boundary: Audience must extend to extra-campus public/government/schools/society; vague mention of "social service" inside an abstract mission motto = <M03>; alumni working in public-sector jobs = <M12>.

12. <M12> Presenting graduate outcomes and alumni development (展示毕业出路与校友发展)
    - Core Question: Where do graduates go after graduation, what careers do they pursue, and how do alumni reflect on their lifelong gains?
    - Includes: Post-graduation career sectors/professions (including "prepare students for careers in..." and "graduates have become doctors, lawyers, CEOs..."), employment/further-study rates, alumni network scale, and named alumni career stories or testimonies.

13. <M13> Projecting future vision and strategic plans (展望未来愿景与建设规划)
    - Core Question: Looking ahead, what future vision, strategic goals, or development initiatives does the institution commit to?
    - Includes: Forward-looking institutional commitments and strategic plans with explicit future orientation ("Looking ahead...", "will continue to build...", "aim to ensure that X continues to be...").
    - Boundary: Requires institutional subject + future orientation; "look forward to welcoming you" = <M14>.

--- DOMAIN V: Reader Engagement, Governance Rules, News, Philanthropy & Tribute ---
14. <M14> Welcoming and prompting reader engagement (致意欢迎与互动邀请)
    - Core Question: How does the text directly greet readers ("you") or invite them to explore, apply, visit, or get in touch?
    - Includes: Direct greetings/warm welcome headings ("A Warm Welcome from...", "Welcome to...!"), warm closing wishes, and explicit reader-directed action prompts ("We warmly invite you to explore...", "Learn more about...", "Please don't hesitate to get in touch...", step-by-step "Explore / Contact / Apply" prompts).
    - Boundary: Fundraising/donation calls ("Give Today", "Make a gift") = <M18>; static office addresses/phones/emails = <M15>; bare UI navigation buttons without sentence punctuation ("About Us", "Meet the Department", "View all individual faculty profiles") = <OTHER>.

15. <M15> Facilitating contact and access coordinates (提供联系与到访信息)
    - Core Question: At what physical address, room, phone, fax, email, office hours, or designated contact person can readers reach the institution?
    - Includes: Office locations, street addresses, room numbers, office hours, phone/fax numbers, email addresses, and designated contact cards with email/phone coordinates.
    - Boundary: Group consecutive lines of a single mailing address / office contact card into ONE <M15>...</M15> unit (unless formatting single-line units); bare "Contact Us" headings = <OTHER>; exact duplicate footers repeated across scraped subpages after the first full occurrence = <OTHER>.

16. <M16> Specifying regulations, procedures and authority boundaries (规定规章程序与权责边界)
    - Core Question: What mandatory eligibility thresholds, credit quotas, administrative procedures, or legal/endorsement disclaimers must be obeyed?
    - Includes: Binding admission/degree requirements ("must", "are required to"), credit minimums, application deadlines/compliance procedures, and official disclaimers ("does not imply endorsement", "should not be taken as a position of the University").
    - Boundary: DO NOT fall for the word "eligibility" when promoting a scholarship/seminar benefit ("enjoy exclusive eligibility for...")—benefits/support = <M08>! <M16> strictly requires binding institutional rules, procedures, or legal disclaimers.

17. <M17> Announcing and reporting specific events and news (播报具体动态与近期活动)
    - Core Question: Which specific dated lecture/event is being announced, or which named recent news item just occurred?
    - Includes: Dated upcoming lectures/seminars/workshops and dated news items reporting specific recent awards, appointments, or book publications.
    - Boundary: Undated cumulative achievements ("hosted many international conferences") = <M09>; obituaries = <M19>; bare "News & Events" menu words = <OTHER>.

18. <M18> Mobilizing philanthropic support and acknowledging donors (动员社会捐赠与致谢支持)
    - Core Question: How does the text invite alumni/public donations, explain gift impact, or thank named donors/endowments?
    - Includes: Donation calls ("Give Today", "Make a gift", "Support the Department..."), statements of how gifts help students ("Your gift fuels opportunities..."), donation channels, and acknowledgements of named donors/endowed funds ("Through the generosity of...").
    - Boundary: Institutional scholarships/fellowships offered to students = <M08>; philanthropic giving/donor gratitude = <M18>.

19. <M19> Commemorating deceased figures and paying tribute (缅怀已故人物与致敬传承)
    - Core Question: How does the community mourn a deceased individual and honor their life and legacy?
    - Includes: Obituaries, "In Memoriam" notices, and tributes strictly for DECEASED HUMAN INDIVIDUALS.
    - Boundary: Commemorating historical event anniversaries (e.g., "50th anniversary of the Vietnam War") is NOT <M19>; general founding history = <M02>.

--- QUALITY-CONTROL FILTER LABEL (Yu et al., 2026) ---
20. <OTHER> Non-propositional web artifacts, unelaborated rosters, and out-of-scope residue (非简介话语残渣与纯导航/名录过滤项)
    - Use <OTHER> whenever none of <M01>–<M19> genuinely applies, specifically:
      (a) Isolated webpage navigation menus, section headings lacking propositional verbs ("About Us", "Contact Us", "Faculty", "Our History", "Meet the Department", "View all individual faculty profiles", alphabetical filter lists such as "- Emeritus"), HTML/image-alt fragments ("black and white image of..."), garbled characters, or collector notes ("无专门about部分");
      (b) Scraped directory rosters consisting purely of person cards without prose sentences (enrolled PhD/MA/undergraduate student lists, student spotlight thumbnail cards with hometown/class year only, or raw paginated name/title directories), as well as repeated sidebar/footer blocks duplicated across scraped subpages;
      (c) Out-of-scope text belonging to a completely non-language department accidentally concatenated into the file OR self-descriptions spoken in the voice ("we") of an external third-party organization (e.g., an external magazine describing itself: "Belt Magazine is a digital publication... we challenge simplistic national narratives...").

======================================================================
LEARN INSTRUCTION 2: STANDARD SENTENCE-BY-SENTENCE ANNOTATION FORMAT
======================================================================
Annotate prose text SENTENCE BY SENTENCE, placing ONE complete sentence per line, with XML tags ONLY at the very start and very end of each complete sentence—NEVER chop a single sentence into mid-sentence fragments:

<M03>We work together to forge intercultural connections.</M03>
<M09>The Department of Languages, Cultures & Applied Linguistics features award-winning teachers and internationally recognized researchers.</M09>
<M14>Welcome to the Department of Languages, Cultures & Applied Linguistics (LCAL)!</M14>
<M14>We warmly invite you to visit our world by exploring our many course offerings, two majors and eight minors and other amazing opportunities to engage with the rich diversity of languages and cultures around us.</M14>
<M05>For students interested in the how of language learning, our second language acquisition courses explore theoretical, technological, cognitive and policy issues, and discuss questions of bilingualism and multilingualism, language diversity and identity.</M05>
<M05>Our offerings in English highlight a wide range of cultural production, from German film to Japanese anime and Chinese TikTok to gaming in French, as well as key topics of race and ethnicity, gender and sexuality, human rights and social justice.</M05>
<M06>Like our advanced language seminars, these courses use the lens of cultural studies to examine other societies and understand diverse perspectives, giving students the tools to think more critically about the world around them and their place in it.</M06>
<M09>No wonder students consistently rate LCAL courses as among their best experiences at Carnegie Mellon!</M09>

======================================================================
LEARN INSTRUCTION 3: COMBINED MOVES ON A SINGLE COMPLETE SENTENCE
======================================================================
Sometimes a single complete sentence genuinely realizes TWO co-equal rhetorical moves simultaneously. Do NOT split the sentence into broken grammatical pieces; keep the entire sentence intact and wrap it with nested tags `<MXX><MYY>...</MYY></MXX>`:

Example A (Co-equal teaching <M06> + research <M07>):
<M06><M07>Our research and teaching promotes language learning, deepens cultural understanding through literary and cultural studies and highlights cutting-edge technology-enhanced language learning.</M07></M06>

Example B (Co-equal graduate career outcomes <M12> + educational cultivation <M06>):
<M12><M06>The reason our graduates have become doctors, authors, venture capitalists, lawyers, and CEOs is because English at Carnegie Mellon ultimately offers equipment for living—the tools for clear and persuasive communication, for deep and patient thinking, for engaged citizenship, and for a fully realized personal and professional life.</M06></M12>

Example C (Co-equal historical milestone <M02> + national first-mover prestige claim <M09>):
<M02><M09>In 1958, the BS in Technical Writing & Editing, the first degree of its kind in the country, was added as a possible major within MMCC under the initiative of Erwin Steinberg.</M09></M02>

======================================================================
LEARN INSTRUCTION 4: DO NOT OVER-COMBINE SUBORDINATE MODIFIERS
======================================================================
Only assign combined tags when BOTH moves carry independent, co-equal propositional weight. When brief phrases ("nine language programs", "two majors and eight minors", "critical thinking") appear merely as subordinate modifiers supporting ONE clear dominant move, assign ONLY the single dominant move:

- Dominant invitation (<M14> ONLY, NOT <M14><M05>):
<M14>We warmly invite you to visit our world by exploring our many course offerings, two majors and eight minors and other amazing opportunities to engage with the rich diversity of languages and cultures around us.</M14>

- Dominant fundraising appeal (<M18> vs <M06>/<M08>—here the prepositional "With..." list leads into empowering students <M06> inside a fundraising box; either code the dominant main clause <M06> or <M06><M08>, never 4 separate tags):
<M18>Support the Department of Languages, Cultures & Applied Linguistics in advancing global understanding.</M18>
<M06><M08>With nine language and culture programs, study abroad opportunities, including guaranteed scholarships for all majors, and cutting-edge tech in VR, AR and podcasting, we empower students to explore and innovate during their time at CMU.</M08></M06>
<M18>Your gift fuels opportunities that shape future leaders in academia, industry and beyond.</M18>

======================================================================
LEARN INSTRUCTION 5: AVOID POSITIONAL BIAS & LEXICAL TRIGGER TRAPS
======================================================================
Moves do NOT appear in a fixed order, and any move may be absent. Watch out for five frequent LLM lexical traps:

1. "eligibility" Trap:
   WRONG: <M16>Graduate students from consortium institutions enjoy exclusive eligibility for the Folger Institute's annual dissertation seminar...</M16>
   CORRECT (promotes a research support benefit, NOT a binding regulation):
   <M08>Graduate students from consortium institutions enjoy exclusive eligibility for the Folger Institute's annual dissertation seminar, a highly regarded program designed to support doctoral research and foster scholarly community among emerging researchers.</M08>

2. "race / gender / social justice" Course Topic Trap:
   Describing courses covering race, gender, and human rights = <M05> (curricular offerings), NOT <M03> (institutional ethical stance), unless the institution itself declares a public moral pledge (e.g., <M03>Today, the Department of Classics at Hopkins repudiates the racist views of Gildersleeve and pledges its efforts to bring the benefits of discovery to the world.</M03>).

3. Historical Timeline vs. Current Offerings vs. News Trap:
   - Chronological past milestones in a History section = <M02>:
     <M02>1980: The Rhetoric program is established in 1980, along with the M.A. in Professional Writing program and the Ph.D. in Rhetoric.</M02>
   - Present-day program summary = <M05>:
     <M05>Today, the English Department features ten rich areas of study: First-Year Writing, Creative Writing, Professional Writing, Technical Writing, Literary and Cultural Studies, Rhetoric, Gender Studies, Humanities Analytics, Film & Visual Media, and Global Communication and Applied Translation.</M05>
   - Undated/cumulative conference hosting pride = <M09> (NOT <M17>):
     <M09>Besides, we successfully hosted many international conferences.</M09>
   - Dated specific news item = <M17>:
     <M17>Ana Arjona named a Faculty Fellow of the Kaplan Humanities Institute for 2022-2023, April 28, 2022</M17>

4. Institutional Scholarships (<M08>) vs. Donor Philanthropy (<M18>):
   - University fellowships/scholarships available to students = <M08>.
   - Thanking named alumni donors or soliciting gifts = <M18>:
     <M18>Through the generosity of Frank ('78) and Cindy Liu, the Liu Distinguished Visitor Series has allowed the Chao Center to host a number of dynamic speakers in 2024-25, including a series commemorating the 50th anniversary of the Vietnam War.</M18> (Note: NOT <M19>, because the Vietnam War is an event, not a deceased person!)

5. External Partner Scope vs. Department's Own Research (<M07>) & Third-Party Voice (<OTHER>):
   - When a consortium library page lists fields supported by the external Folger Institute (including medicine, religion, art history), code as <M08> (external research support resource), NEVER <M07>.
   - When an external partner publication speaks in its own first-person voice ("we" = Belt Magazine, NOT the university department), code as <OTHER>:
     <OTHER>Belt Magazine is a digital publication by and for the Rust Belt and greater Midwest.</OTHER>
     <OTHER>Founded in 2013 as an antidote to shallow, distorted representations of the region, we challenge simplistic national narratives by paying local journalists, writers, photographers, and poets to cover their communities with depth.</OTHER>

======================================================================
LEARN INSTRUCTION 6: HANDLING HEADINGS, SIGNATURES, CONTACTS & LISTS
======================================================================
Webpage texts contain non-sentence structural elements alongside prose sentences. Handle them with strict consistency:
1. Empty generic section headings, breadcrumbs, navigation menus, directory filter lists, and bare student/staff directory cards -> <OTHER>...</OTHER> (keep multi-line cards/menus together or one line per item, preserving exact original wording).
2. Headings that explicitly perform a rhetorical move (e.g., `<M14>A Warm Welcome from Department Head, Anne Lambright</M14>`, `<M18>Give Today</M18>`, `<M02>Over 100 Years of English at Carnegie Mellon</M02>`) -> tag with that move.
3. Structured contact address blocks -> wrap the address/phone/hours block as `<M15>...</M15>`; leader sign-offs at the end of a welcome message (`Name + Title`) -> wrap together as ONE `<M01>...</M01>` block (NEVER tag a person's bare name alone as a move without their institutional title/role!).

======================================================================
FINAL EXECUTION TASK
======================================================================
Please remember everything you have learned, take a deep breath, check who "we" / the subject refers to in every section, pay careful attention to dominant moves vs. genuine combined moves (`<MXX><MYY>...</MYY></MXX>`), and annotate EVERY unit in the following text using ONLY `<M01>`–`<M19>` or `<OTHER>`:

STRICT OUTPUT RULES:
1. Preserve 100% of the original words, spelling, and punctuation verbatim—DO NOT omit, summarize, translate, or alter any original text.
2. In prose paragraphs, place ONE complete sentence per line, with move tags ONLY at the beginning and end of each complete sentence (NEVER split mid-sentence).
3. For multi-line structured blocks (e.g., one mailing address block, one `Name + Title` signature block, or one repeated sidebar menu), keep the cohesive block wrapped cleanly in a single `<TAG>...</TAG>` pair.
4. Output ONLY the XML-annotated text. Do not generate explanations, commentary, markdown code fences, or any other content.

### INPUT TEXT TO ANNOTATE:
[PASTE RAW UNIVERSITY .TXT CONTENT HERE]

```
